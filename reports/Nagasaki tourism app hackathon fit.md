# Cut five features, keep one connection

**Verdict: as pitched, the idea is not novel and will score poorly on originality. A trimmed version can still win.** Each of the six parts already exists. Most are run by Google, Niantic or Nagasaki Prefecture itself, and the prefecture is one of the judges. The Poké Lid stamp rally is the worst overlap: the prefecture runs it live during the hackathon. The AI shop/hotel chatbot is the second worst: Google Maps added Gemini "Ask Maps" in Japanese in August 2026. Two red lines also apply. Pokémon assets are unlicensed IP. Gamifying the Peace Park offends local norms, and Nagasaki has asked Niantic for removals there before. The open gap is narrow but real, and it fits the TSUNAGU "connect" theme. Nobody runs a multilingual layer where **Nagasaki locals recommend their meibutsu (local specialities) and the shops that sell them**, aimed at the day-trippers and cruise passengers who spend least. Build that one loop well. Treat the peace content as quiet learning, not a game.

## Google Maps wins on discovery but has no local voices

Nobody will switch from Google Maps to find a hotel or a ramen shop. Google has the place data, the reviews, live transit and now Gemini itineraries in Japanese. A student chatbot built on hand-collected data loses on every axis, so do not compete there.

Google does not model **who in Nagasaki recommends what, and why**. Rankings list what to buy. They do not say which nearby shop sells it, which local stands behind the pick, or what the story is (kasutera, karasumi, Dejima). The city's own data shows why this matters. **64% of Nagasaki City's 6.9M visitors in 2025 were day-trippers**, spending ¥22,400 each against ¥52,150 for overnight guests. About 70% of foreign visitors were day-trippers, mostly cruise passengers ([city statistics 2025](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/63554.pdf)). **Spend per cruise passenger fell to ¥18,828**, and souvenir and shopping spend is dropping ([DMO material](https://www.at-nagasaki.jp/downloads/media/15983)). The city DMO also names these problems: too few half-day experiences, weak online booking for small operators, and a multilingual restaurant tool that struggles to stay updated ([DMO report](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/67552.pdf)). So the honest answer to "why not Google Maps" is narrow. You would use this app *alongside* Google for one job: a 4-hour port stop with trusted local picks and a direct route to small shops, in English and Korean. Korea is the top inbound market at 27% of foreign nights. The official rally page is Japanese-only.

Two weaknesses remain. First, a community needs both locals and tourists before it is useful. Ask Japan has only 3 App Store ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ask-japan-local-travel-q-a/id6748002901)). Pitch a seeding plan, such as local students and shopkeepers. Second, no source measures whether stamp rallies change where tourists go. Claim "check-in data the DMO can use", not "proven behaviour change". The DMO says it collects data that businesses do not use ([DMO report](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/67552.pdf)).

## Two red lines: no Pokémon assets, no points at the Peace Park

**Pokémon.** The Pokémon Company forbids secondary and commercial use of its material ([Pokémon Center terms](https://www.pokemoncenter-online.com/feature/digital-contents-terms.html)). In March 2026 it publicly disowned even the US White House's Pokémon-style images ([GameBusiness](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/03/07/26363.html)). A public GitHub repo plus a demo video counts as public distribution. Do not use the Pokémon or ポケふた name, sprites, lid photos or scraped lid lists. At most, link out to the official rally. If you mention Nagasaki's partner Pokémon at all, it is Ampharos (デンリュウ), not Lapras ([Kyodo PR](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202406282888)). This is not legal advice.

**Peace Park.** In 2016 Nagasaki asked Niantic to remove Pokémon GO stops. The sources disagree on scope. One says park curators asked about the whole park because it is "a place to pray" ([SoraNews24](https://soranews24.com/2016/07/27/nagasakis-peace-park-asks-pokemon-go-developer-to-remove-game-functionality-on-park-grounds/)). Another says the city asked only about the Atomic Bomb Museum area ([ITmedia](https://nlab.itmedia.co.jp/cont/articles/3257599/)). Either way, use no stamps, points, streaks or confetti at the hypocentre or the Peace Park. Never show the Peace Statue as a collectible. The statue's sculptor, Kitamura Seibō, died in 1987, so a 3D replica likely raises copyright issues as well. This is unverified.

**3D is a build trap too.** PLATEAU has no 3D city model for Nagasaki City. In the prefecture it covers only Sasebo, Matsuura and Hasami ([MLIT PLATEAU](https://www.mlit.go.jp/plateau/open-data/)). No licensed Peace Statue model was found. Cut the 3D park, or use a simple 2D "learn" pin that links to testimony.

**AI chatbot.** If you keep one, ground it only in your own curated shop data. It must cite the entry it used and say "I don't know" otherwise, because invented opening hours or allergy information hurt trust.

## The 3-day MVP: one loop, three screens

Build one loop: **a local pins a meibutsu pick, a visitor finds it on a map, walks there and checks in, and the shop sees the visit.**

On day 1, build the map (MapLibre + OSM, or the Google free tier) with 20–30 hand-checked Nagasaki shops and spots. Each one gets a real source URL, a meibutsu tag and an EN/JA/KO blurb. Clearly label any sample data. On day 2, build local-pick posts and check-in by GPS radius with a QR fallback. Check-ins earn "Nagasaki passport" stamps, with no Pokémon branding and no memorial sites. On day 3, build a small heatmap dashboard of check-ins (the "data for the DMO and shops" story), polish, and record the demo. Only if time remains, add a chatbot that answers from the same JSON with citations. Cut the 3D park, the hotel search, the separate souvenir map and the Poké Lids. Add a link to 「ながさき照らす旅3」 so the app complements the prefecture's rally instead of copying it.

**Pitch line:** *"Google tells you where. Nagasaki locals tell you what's worth it. We connect a 4-hour cruise visitor to the shop a local would send their own family to, and give the city the data on where visitors went."*

## Estimated scores on the 5 criteria

| Criterion | As pitched | Trimmed MVP | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem definition & theme fit | 2/5 | 4/5 | The trimmed version targets a documented city problem (day-trip and cruise spend) and literally connects locals with visitors. |
| Technical skill | 3/5 | 3–4/5 | A 3D model and AI look impressive but would be shallow. A working geofenced check-in, QR fallback, heatmap and grounded chatbot are solid and demoable. |
| Completeness | 1–2/5 | 4/5 | Six features in 3 days ship half-built. One loop ships whole. |
| Originality | 1/5 | 3/5 | Every pitched part has an incumbent, and the judges run one of them. A locals' meibutsu layer has no Nagasaki incumbent, though the community and passport mechanics are familiar. |
| Proposal clarity | 2/5 | 4/5 | "Why not Google Maps / the prefecture rally" gets a direct answer. |

These scores are judgement calls, not data. The biggest swing factor is a demo with real check-ins the team makes in Nagasaki during the event.

## Conclusion

The idea fails as a "super app", but its instinct is sound. The team's best asset is the one feature it listed least prominently, the locals-and-tourists community, because it is the only part that no incumbent owns in Nagasaki and the only part that carries the TSUNAGU theme. Pitch it as a complement to the prefecture's rally and to Google Maps, aimed at the city's own documented spend gap. That turns the prefecture judges from people who notice a copy into people who see a partner.

One uncertainty remains. No source shows that locals will post, or that check-ins change spending. Judges will probe the cold-start plan more than the code. Answer with a concrete seeding plan, not with more features.
