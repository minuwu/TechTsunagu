# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is small and has clear edges.** It is factories in one prefecture that employ 特定技能 and 技能実習 workers from a few countries. That is the kind of market one product can own before it expands. Bengali support, which general manual tools are unlikely to prioritise, narrows it further.
- **The payer has a legal reason to pay.** For 特定技能1号, registered support organisations must provide support by law. If the Support Log counts as evidence of that support, the factory gets the app for free and the payer gets a compliance product. The app reaches factories through a channel that already visits them.
- **Some lock-in could build up.** Each factory builds a library of machine instructions and a sign-off history, which makes it harder to leave. Across many factories, the record of misunderstood shop terms (玉掛け, 段取り) could become a dataset that no general translator has.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A copycat ships it in month six.** tebiki, Teachme Biz and カミナシ already translate manuals and run quizzes, and they may already sell to the same buyer. The machine lock and the alert that names the misunderstood term are features, not a big technical lead. An incumbent with customers and a sales team could add a "gate by machine" screen quickly. Nothing here is roughly ten times better on something that cannot be copied.
2. **The money comes from someone else, and the distribution is unproven.** The factory uses the app, but the support organisation pays, and its price is not set. There is no evidence yet that any support organisation would pay, or that its regulator would accept the log. Without that, there is no route to the first 100 customers and no known cost per customer.
3. **The core technology is turning into a commodity.** Translation, plain Japanese and quiz generation run on a third-party model that every competitor can use too, and it improves for all of them at the same rate. The model's free tier allows about 20 calls a day, so the current setup cannot scale.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many target factories and support organisations are there in Nagasaki? What share could you hold in year one? (Not known yet.)
- What do support organisations pay today for these duties? Would any of them sign up at a named price?
- Would the Immigration Services Agency, or an auditor, accept the Support Log as evidence of 支援?
- Do tebiki, Teachme Biz or カミナシ already sell to these factories or support organisations?
- What do you know that they do not, and why can't they copy it?
- Would a plant manager let a software lock stand in front of a machine, and who is liable if a worker ignores it?
- Why now? What changed in 2025–2026 that makes this possible or urgent?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A letter of intent from at least one support organisation that names a price and confirms the log fits its legal support duties.
2. One Nagasaki factory agrees to pilot the lock panel on a real machine.
3. A sized beachhead: the number of target factories and support organisations, and the share planned for year one.
4. A written moat plan for building the cross-factory dataset of misunderstood terms into something an incumbent cannot copy within a year, plus an AI cost model that works beyond the free tier.
