# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain is real and on record.** 46.2% of employers name language as their top challenge with foreign workers, and the share is rising (MHLW 2025).
- **The payer has a legal reason to buy.** Support organisations must provide life orientation, consultation and exchange support for 特定技能1号 workers. A log that proves this was done ties into a duty they already have. That is stronger than a nice-to-have.
- **It asks little of the worker.** A QR sticker, no install, and their own language. Results show up within minutes.
- **The difference is not price.** The machine lock and the alert that names the misunderstood term are things tebiki, Teachme Biz and カミナシ do not claim. That gives the offer something to stand on besides being free.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nobody has shown the payer will pay.** No price is set, nobody has been asked, and nobody knows what support organisations spend on these duties today. Worse, the payer is the support organisation but the people doing the work are the factory's foremen. The log only has value if foremen fill the app every day. If they don't, the support organisation gets an empty log and stops paying.
2. **Nothing proves it works.** There are no pilots and no customers, and the injury figures are not yet verified. Nobody knows whether a factory will accept a software panel in front of a machine, or whether an inspector will accept the Support Log as evidence. Each "no" removes half the offer.
3. **The free side carries the most effort.** Foremen record, check and re-approve every machine, and every edit voids the old sign-offs. Factories pay nothing, so they have no commitment and every reason to drop it in a busy week. Running the AI on about 20 calls a day also means the result cannot be delivered at the scale of one real factory.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which support organisation, by name, told you what it pays today for orientation and consultation, and what it would pay per worker for this?
- Does the Support Log match, field by field, what a support organisation must actually file? Who has confirmed that?
- What does the factory get that makes saying no feel stupid, when it isn't the one paying?
- What guarantee would you offer? For example: "if the log does not cover your required support records, you pay nothing."
- How many support organisations and target factories are there in Nagasaki? (Unknown. This is an open question.)
- Is the foreign-worker population on these visas growing in Kyushu? The brief gives no figure. This is an open question.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. At least three support organisations give a stated price, or a letter of intent, for the Support Log, along with what they spend on the same duties today.
2. One factory agrees to put the lock on one real machine, and its foremen keep recording for a set trial period.
3. The Support Log is checked against the actual legal filing requirements by someone who files them.
4. The pitch leads with one stack aimed at the payer (log, notices and Senpai, with the machine lock as the bonus that keeps factories using it) and offers a guarantee tied to the compliance outcome.
