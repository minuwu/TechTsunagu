# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
- One moment is clear and vivid. A worker scans the QR code, sees LOCK, reads the instruction in Bengali, passes the quiz, and the panel turns READY. That works as a demo and it tells a story.
- The wrong-answer alert that names the exact shop word (玉掛け, 段取り) is a detail with taste. It turns a failed quiz into something the foreman can act on, not just a score.
- The back-translation lets a foreman check Bengali he cannot read. It solves a real problem at the point where the handoff would otherwise break.
- No install, a QR code on each machine, and the worker's own phone. The first touch is cheap.
## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No single thing.** This is two products: machine-gated shop training, and neighbourhood life support (garbage, drills, notices Q&A, Senpai). On top of that sit a skill matrix, a heatmap and a Support Log. The best moment is on the factory floor, but the payer's legal duty (life orientation, consultation, exchange with Japanese people) sits mostly in the home half. That pulls the product in two directions, and it cannot do both brilliantly. The pitch takes a paragraph, so the product is not clear yet.
2. **The lock is a promise the product may not keep.** It is a software panel, not an interlock. If a worker can walk past LOCK, the core experience is decoration. If a factory will not stand a tablet in front of a machine (unknown), the centre of the product disappears. A wrong translation that a worker then "passes" is worse than having no gate at all.
3. **The foreman's first five minutes.** The foreman is the busiest person in the chain. He records 20 seconds of speech and must get back an approvable trilingual draft, correct safety icons and sensible quiz questions. Shop jargon in Japanese is where AI drafts tend to go wrong. A free tier of about 20 calls a day means the demo may not survive a real shift. Each approval also voids every sign-off on that machine, which adds friction exactly where adoption is decided.
## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: what does this do that a factory cannot live without?
- If you could keep only the machine half or only the neighbourhood half, which would you keep, and does the support organisation still pay for it?
- Have you watched a real foreman record and approve one instruction? How long did it take, and how many edits did he make?
- What happens when a worker ignores LOCK? Who notices, and how?
- Where does the Support Log touch the support organisation's real workflow, and what do they do with it today?
- How did the AI do on 玉掛け and 段取り in your tests, and what was the error rate?
## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut to one product with a one-sentence pitch, and set the other half aside until the first half is loved.
2. Show the full foreman-to-worker loop on one real machine with one real foreman, with timing and edit counts recorded.
3. Get one factory and one support organisation to confirm in writing that the chosen half matches what they would actually use and pay for.
