# Nagasaki tourism: documented problems, numbers and digital initiatives (fact base for TSUNAGU idea check)

Research date: 2026-11-22. Several pref.nagasaki.jp / pref.nagasaki.lg.jp PDFs returned HTTP 404 when fetched (site migration), so some prefecture figures come from search-result extracts of those PDFs and are marked "(search extract)". Nagasaki City figures come from the full text of two official PDFs (2025 city statistics, FY2025 DMO report) and are the most reliable items here.

## 1. Visitor numbers 2023-2026 (prefecture and city)

### Takeaway
Nagasaki is growing, not struggling. The prefecture had about 30.8M visits and ¥458.7B spend in 2024. Reports say spend set a record of about ¥504.5B in 2025. Nagasaki City had 6.9M visitors in 2025, and 64% of them were day-trippers. Inbound demand is led by Korea and is rising fast. Cruise ships made 197 international calls at Nagasaki port in 2025 and brought about 519k passengers and crew.

### Cited Findings
**Prefecture**
- 2024: total visits (延べ, including day trips) were 30,804,676 (+2.4%). Overnight stays (延べ宿泊客数) were 7,483,382 (+2.9%, 4th straight rise). Tourism spend was ¥458.7B (+15.5%). The prefecture credits new hotels, Nagasaki Stadium City, big sports events and restored international air routes. (search extract of 令和6年長崎県観光統計, published 2025-09-16) — [Pref PDF](https://www.pref.nagasaki.lg.jp/shared/uploads/2025/09/1757982394.pdf)
- 2023: overnight stays were 7.32M (+15.9%) and total visits were 29.35M (+16.3%). — [長崎新聞 2024-09-20](https://www.nagasaki-np.co.jp/kijis/?kijiid=c623bb89260445a8ade246cfd4578958) (search extract)
- 2024 foreign overnight stays: about 601k person-nights (preliminary), about 1.3x 2023. The prefecture says regional areas, Nagasaki included, recovered more slowly than the three big metro areas. (search extract) — [Pref 観光振興財源検討専門委員会 2025](https://www.pref.nagasaki.lg.jp/shared/uploads/2025/10/1760706196.pdf)
- 2025 (JTA confirmed): hotel/ryokan guests were 7.65M person-nights (+0.3%). Foreign guests were 0.76M (+18.9%). — [長崎新聞 via Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/68ac6fbbc851611bd5eb009c41b6b27d50e6f87e) (headline/snippet only, not fetched)
- 2025: visitors rose for a 5th straight year, with record spend of ¥504.5B. International cruise calls in the prefecture were 334 (+38.6%). In 2026, Chinese visitors are falling, Chinese-origin cruises are being cancelled, and the prefecture is focusing on other markets. — [西日本新聞 via Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/6b08f2a1320f6fb9638eb10f0eea1c0ea9d958ab) (UNVERIFIED: headline and search snippet only, page 404 on fetch)
- Sept 2025: overnight stays were 771,280 (-1.9% YoY). Foreign overnight stays were 53,780 (+36.3%). — [ながさき旅ネット data](https://www.nagasaki-tabinet.com/downloads/media/150921) (search extract)
- Q4 2025 (Oct-Dec): guests at main lodgings were +5.9% and users of main attractions were +14.4%. Drivers were events, more international ferry/air services, and more Korean guests. — [Pref 観光動向調査 2026-03-03](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2026/03/1772515804.pdf) (search extract)

**Nagasaki City, 2025 final figures** (source for every item in this block: [令和7年長崎市観光統計 確報版](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/63554.pdf), full text read)
- Visitors: 6,902,993 (+5.5%). Overnight visitors: 2,501,437 (36.2%). Day-trippers: 4,401,556 (63.8%).
- Japanese visitors: 6,014,785. Foreign visitors: 888,208 (12.9%, +9.8%). Foreign day-trippers: 624,315, about 70% of foreign visitors.
- Cruise passengers vs crew (derived by summing the monthly chart on p.14 of the PDF): 375,054 passengers and 144,798 crew, total 519,852. 375k passengers is about 60% of the 624k foreign day-trippers. Treat the 60% as a derived estimate.
- Person-nights: 3,149,506 in total. Japanese: 2,685,054. Foreign: 464,452 (+27.4%, vs 323k in 2019).
- Spend: ¥229.0B in total (Japanese ¥183.3B, foreign ¥45.7B). Economic ripple effect in the prefecture: ¥344.0B.
- Spend per person: average ¥33,180. Overnight visitor ¥52,150, day-tripper ¥22,400. Japanese ¥30,479, foreign ¥51,475.
- Cruise: 198 calls (+23.8%) with 519,852 passengers and crew (+10.1%). Of these, 197 international calls carried 518,851. The 2024 figures were 160 calls and 472,120. The 2023 figures were 96 calls and 167,086.
- Cruise seasonality: the peak was March with 26 calls (~70k people), then April with 24. The low was December with 6 calls (~19k).
- Visitor seasonality is fairly flat. The peak was August at 684,505 (9.9%), then November at 670,067 (9.7%). The low was January at 484,465 (7.0%).
- Foreign person-nights by origin, 2025:
  - Korea 124,938 (26.9%)
  - Taiwan 47,839 (10.3%)
  - USA 46,910 (10.1%)
  - China 46,445 (10.0%)
  - Singapore 18,578
  - Hong Kong 16,256
  - Germany 14,862
  - Australia 13,469
  - UK 12,540
  - France and Canada 8,825 each
- By region: Asia 61%, Europe 14%, Americas 13%.
- School trips (修学旅行) visitors: 248,100 (-4.1%), a long-term decline from 297,300 in 2015.
- Method break: from 2024 the city estimates with lodging-tax returns plus people-flow data (おでかけウォッチャー GPS for Japanese, docomo モバイル空間統計 for foreigners). Figures before 2023 are not directly comparable.
- Drivers named for 2025:
  - inbound growth;
  - Stadium City fully operating: MICE events up to 1,980 (+24.1%), participants 1.86M, sports-convention participants +112.5%;
  - **Pokémon GO Wild Area: Nagasaki (Nov 7-9, 2025): about 420,000 players according to the organizer**, cited as one reason November visitors rose 9.8%;
  - Nagasaki Peace Culture Festival 2025;
  - 80th anniversary of the A-bomb: Atomic Bomb Museum visitors +10.9%.
- Spend per cruise passenger in the city fell from ¥19,757 (2023 survey) to ¥18,828 (2024 survey, n=608). Souvenir/shopping spend is falling while satisfaction is rising. Cruise visitors are going less to the A-bomb museum and Peace Park and more to Chinatown and the history museum. — [at-nagasaki DMO material](https://www.at-nagasaki.jp/downloads/media/15983) (search extract)

### Inferences
- Foreign day-trippers are mostly cruise passengers, about 375k of the 624k. Roughly ¥18.8k each means about ¥7B/yr of cruise spend in the city.
- The city's core economic gap is day-tripper versus overnight spend: ¥22,400 against ¥52,150. Nearly 2 of 3 visitors do not stay overnight. Among foreigners, about 70% are day-trippers, largely cruise passengers who spend under ¥19k.
- Korea is the top inbound market by a wide margin. China is now volatile (2026 cruise cancellations). English plus Korean are the key app languages, with Traditional Chinese next.

### Gaps
- Prefecture 2025 annual statistics (expected around Sept 2026): not retrieved.
- **The split by municipality or island (Goto/Iki/Tsushima versus Nagasaki City, Sasebo/Huis Ten Bosch) is NOT in these notes.** Both the prefecture PDF and the Excel index page [長崎県観光統計データ](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/bunrui/kanko-kyoiku-bunka/kanko-bussan/statistics/kankoutoukei/296549.html) returned 404. The idea that visitors concentrate on the main spots while the islands get too few is therefore unproven here. The 市町別 table in [長崎県統計年鑑 令和7年版](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/doc/44979.html) is another possible source (not checked).
- **World Heritage: not researched (budget).** Missing: visitor numbers and access issues for the Hidden Christian Sites (many on Goto/Hirado/Sotome, reachable only by ferry or limited buses), the Meiji Industrial Revolution sites, and Gunkanjima landing tours (weather-dependent landing rate). No figures here.
- Prefecture-level inbound breakdown by country for 2025: not found. (Tsushima depends heavily on Korean ferry visitors. That is general knowledge here, not sourced.)
- I could not verify the ¥504.5B and 334-cruise-call figures beyond the headline.

## 2. Problems named in official documents

### Takeaway
The documented problems are short stays and low local spend (day trips, cruises), too few half-day and food experiences, too few guides (ageing, non-English), weak online booking for small operators, limited multilingual support at restaurants, and falling satisfaction with information while travelling (旅ナカ) for Japanese visitors. Official sources do not frame the problem as overtourism, and in fact they push for more visitors. Note: the city DMO says data is collected but local businesses do not use it.

### Cited Findings
All of these come from Nagasaki City's FY2025 DMO report (令和7年度 長崎市DMO事業報告書, published June 2026), full text read — [PDF](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/67552.pdf):
- Content: the line-up of experiences that cruise passengers and Kyushu-touring travellers can join in half a day to a day "is still limited". Food experiences are relatively few. Most bookings come by direct inquiry because "an environment where booking completes online is not sufficiently developed", which limits sales.
- Inbound activity participation: Japanese food 69.0%, town walking 63.2%, history/culture 44.3%, nature/rural experiences only 10.1%, daily-life experiences 18.5%. The report says inbound experience content is insufficient.
- Guides:
  - ageing guides are a problem;
  - registered peace/history guides for school trips: 138 (-8.6%);
  - satisfaction with guides is only 17.2% "very satisfied";
  - English paid guides (Nagasaki Crew) number 44;
  - there is a shortage of guides who can serve VIPs or specialist themes;
  - businesses also want help with Chinese.
- Restaurants: inbound "food satisfaction" fell to 78.8% (-3.2pt). Restaurants with vegan or Muslim-friendly options are limited. The multilingual restaurant tool "EAT NAGASAKI" struggles to keep data updated and to get businesses to use it. The city surveyed 193 restaurants on how ready they are for inbound visitors.
- Information while travelling (旅ナカ): Japanese satisfaction with the tourist information centre fell to 60.4% (-5.7pt), well below target. Japanese "satisfaction with information while travelling" is well below target and lower than foreigners'. Foreign satisfaction with the information centre is 81.0%.
- Web traffic: travel nagasaki traffic is falling because of zero-click and generative-AI search. play nagasaki PV fell to 146,514 (74.1% of the previous year). The DMO is pivoting to "AIO" (generative-AI search optimisation) and structured data as an "AI-era information platform".
- Data: survey data is gathered but not used enough by businesses or on the ground. It is "specialised and hard to understand". The DMO wants "usable data" such as forecasts of events, conferences and cruise arrivals.
- Visitor wishes: "more experience menus" 11.5% and "more night-time tourism" 11.2%.
- City tourism/MICE council: cruise passengers "just wander the town; we are not driving consumption". The council calls for experience tours, content creation and guide training. Its earlier city plan (2020) proposed night-view content to lengthen port stays. — [長崎市観光・MICE振興審議会](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/page/42021.html); [長崎市観光振興計画2020](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/18982.pdf) (search extracts)
- Prefecture (May 2025 planning material): the prefecture uses length of stay (average nights) as a key lever for spend. It wants to "create appeal that makes people want to stay" and to promote touring across the prefecture (周遊). It notes that Nagasaki has fewer night-time and early-morning attractions than other prefectures. (search extract) — [Pref PDF](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2025/06/1749798364.pdf)

### Inferences
- The problem with the strongest documentation is converting day-trippers and cruise passengers (about 4.4M day visits in the city, cruise spend about ¥18.8k) into longer, wider and higher-spend visits, especially in half-day slots. Shop and food discovery plus multilingual help are named sub-problems.
- "Concentration in a few spots versus islands" fits the prefecture's 周遊 goal, but I found no numeric island data to prove it.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a direct prefecture statement on concentration (Nagasaki City/Huis Ten Bosch versus Goto/Iki/Tsushima) or overtourism. I found no evidence that Nagasaki officially reports overtourism.
- Small-shop digitisation rates: none found, apart from a small e-money pilot (12 shops, 83 payments, ¥213,880, 169 participants, 44% usage) in the DMO report.

## 3. Official plans, KPIs, DX and data initiatives

### Takeaway
The new prefecture plan (2026-2030) has KPIs on spend per person and overnight stays. The city DMO already runs an AI chatbot on travel nagasaki, a booking platform (play nagasaki), a multilingual restaurant tool (EAT NAGASAKI), and people-flow data. The prefecture has a data platform with a public API. A generic "AI chatbot for tourists" is therefore not new in Nagasaki. Helping small businesses use data and AI is a stated gap.

### Cited Findings
- **長崎県観光振興基本計画 2026-2030** (FY2026-2030) replaces the plan that ended in FY2025. Its parent plan is the 長崎県総合計画, and its policy is "観光客の心をつかみ、選ばれる持続可能な観光の推進". KPIs (search extract):
  - spend per visitor: ¥42,884 in FY2024, target ¥44,100 in FY2030;
  - overnight stays: 7.48M person-nights in FY2024, target 8.10M in FY2030, with separate domestic and inbound targets.
  - Background named: population decline, ageing, and the shift to individual travel. — [Pref plan PDF](https://www.pref.nagasaki.lg.jp/shared/uploads/2025/12/1765201620.pdf) (404 on fetch, extract only)
- Data platform 「つながる長崎」: the prefecture and its 21 municipalities link disaster and tourism data and publish it as APIs, mainly for private businesses. The budget includes "people-flow data provision ¥5,280k". (search extract) — [Pref PDF](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2025/10/1761792218.pdf)
- Nagasaki City piloted tourism DX with the MaaS app "STLOCAL" at the 2024 Lantern Festival to collect visitor-movement data. (search extract) — [City PDF](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/27342.pdf)
- The city DMO runs:
  - the **AI chatbot on travel nagasaki** (also used as an FAQ on information-centre tablets);
  - digital signage;
  - play nagasaki (114 listings; 292,082 experience participants; ¥1.33B sales in FY2025);
  - EAT NAGASAKI multilingual restaurant info;
  - help for operators to list on OTAs.
  - travel nagasaki had about 2.51M UU and 6.6M PV in FY2025. Instagram grew from 25,651 to 39,607 followers.
  - A new 5-year DMO plan was being drafted in FY2025.
  - Source: [DMO report](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/67552.pdf)
- The city's Stadium City regional-revitalisation plan targets +¥61.6B tourism spend. (search extract) — [City PDF](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/life/87175_610419_misc.pdf)
- Hackathons: a 2018 prefecture document describes app ideas (night view plus tourist guidance) being built through hackathons. (search extract) — [Pref 2018 PDF](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2018/04/1523949102.pdf). I found no current tourism open-data hackathon.

### Inferences
- A pitch that says "we built a chatbot for tourists" duplicates the travel nagasaki chatbot. A stronger angle is to answer the DMO's stated gaps:
  - a chatbot and booking/inquiry tool that small shops or hotels can set up themselves, in Korean and English;
  - letting businesses read data in simple form;
  - half-day itineraries for cruise passengers, timed to ship schedules.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full plan text (strategies, inbound targets by market, DX section).
- I found no 観光オープンデータ catalogue URL for tourism POIs or Pokémon covers.

## 4. New attractions: Stadium City and the Pokémon partnership

### Takeaway
**Nagasaki's Pokémon is Ampharos (デンリュウ), not Lapras.** Nagasaki Prefecture **already runs a GPS digital stamp rally at its ポケふた and about 40 spots**. It is called 「ながさき照らす旅」, and its 3rd edition runs from Aug 27, 2026 to Feb 28, 2027. The team's "gamified stamp collecting at ポケふた" idea already exists as a live government service.

### Cited Findings
- Nagasaki Prefecture signed a partnership with The Pokémon Company in June 2024 and named **Ampharos (デンリュウ) the "ながさき未来応援ポケモン"**. — [Kyodo PR wire 2024-06-28](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202406282888)
- Every ポケふた in Nagasaki shows Ampharos. In July 2025 there were 5 municipalities: Nagasaki, Sasebo, Omura, Unzen and Shin-Kamigoto. — [Kyodo PR 2025-07-22](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202507222532) (search extract)
- In Aug 2026 there were **15 municipalities with ポケふた**, one cover each per rurubu Kids. 4 of them are on remote islands. More are planned for autumn 2026 in Shimabara, Iki, Togitsu, Higashisonogi and Saza. — [Kyodo PR 2026-08-26](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202608264772); [るるぶKids](https://kids.rurubu.jp/article/243208/) (search extract)
- **「ながさき照らす旅3」** details:
  - a digital stamp rally across ポケふた plus about 40 tourist spots in 21 municipalities;
  - GPS check-in (QR in the 2025 edition), missions, and a lottery for Ampharos goods and local specialities;
  - the first 2,000 people with 2 or more colour stamps get a keychain at 15 facilities;
  - runs Aug 27, 2026 to Feb 28, 2027;
  - the 2024 edition was the first.
  - The release reports **no participant or visitor-effect numbers**. — [Kyodo PR 2026-08-26](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202608264772)
- Pokémon GO Wild Area: Nagasaki (Nov 7-9, 2025) drew about 420k players according to the organizer. It was cited as a driver of the +9.8% rise in November city visitors. — [City statistics 2025](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/63554.pdf)
- Nagasaki Stadium City (opened Oct 2024): Japanet says 4.7M visitors in year 1 and **5.35M projected in year 2**. Funai Soken estimates the economic effect at **¥37.83B/yr**. Both are operator or commissioned estimates. — [Oricon PR](https://www.oricon.co.jp/pressrelease/3020752/). The prefecture credits it with lifting out-of-prefecture sports-spectator stays. — [Pref 観光動向調査](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2025/09/1758246546.pdf) (search extract)
- From my background knowledge only, not verified in this session: Lapras is Miyagi Prefecture's supporting Pokémon.

### Inferences
- If the team builds a ポケふた stamp app, judges from the prefecture will recognise it as a copy of their own campaign. Either drop it, or position the app as a complement to 「ながさき照らす旅」. For example, use rally participants' visits to send people into nearby local shops and specialities, which the rally does not do. Use Ampharos, never Lapras.
- Pokémon IP is licensed. A student app cannot use Pokémon artwork or the ポケふた brand without permission.

### Gaps
- Exact cover locations per municipality.
- Participation or visitor-flow results of the 2024 and 2025 rallies. A prefecture survey PDF exists ([1763107646.pdf](https://www.pref.nagasaki.jp/shared/uploads/2025/11/1763107646.pdf)) but returned 404.

## 5. Sensitivities around the atomic-bomb sites

### Takeaway
Nagasaki City treats the Atomic Bomb Museum as a place for quiet learning. In 2016 it asked Niantic to remove Pokémon GO stops in and around the museum. Gamifying or commercialising the Peace Park or the Peace Statue is risky in front of prefecture judges. A respectful 3D model for learning is less risky than stamps or points there.

### Cited Findings
- In 2016 Nagasaki City applied to Niantic to remove PokéStops inside and around the Atomic Bomb Museum. The reason was danger from crowding and walking while looking at phones. It did not then request removal for the rest of the Peace Park, including the area around the Peace Statue. Hiroshima asked for removal across Peace Memorial Park "out of consideration for worshippers and peace-purpose visitors". — [ITmedia ねとらぼ](https://nlab.itmedia.co.jp/cont/articles/3257599/); [Response](https://s.response.jp/article/2016/07/29/279204.html)
- The city uses peace and A-bomb learning programmes for school trips (ながさき平和・歴史ガイド, 25,546 participants in FY2025, -21%). — [DMO report](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/67552.pdf)
- Atomic Bomb Museum visitors rose 10.9% in 2025, the 80th anniversary year. — [City statistics 2025](https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/63554.pdf)

### Inferences
- Exclude the Peace Park and the hypocentre from any stamp or game mechanics. Present them only in an educational mode, for example 3D plus testimony links.

### Gaps
- I found no current written city guideline on games or commerce at peace sites, and did not check whether those PokéStops exist today.
- I did not check the 3D-model copyright status of the Peace Statue (sculptor Seibō Kitamura, died 1987, so the work is likely still under copyright in Japan until 2057). Unverified, but relevant before 3D-modelling it.
